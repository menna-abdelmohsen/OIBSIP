Sameera Moussa

سميرة موسى

Egypt's first female nuclear scientist, who wanted atomic science to heal people.

Who she was

Sameera Moussa (also spelled Samira Moussa) was born on 3 March 1917 in Zifta, in Egypt's Gharbia governorate. She studied at Cairo University, where she became the first woman to hold a teaching post, and the first Egyptian woman to earn a doctorate in atomic radiation.

She is often called the "Miss Curie of the East". Her doctoral research looked at how different materials absorb X-rays. Her professor and mentor was the Egyptian scientist Ali Mostafa Mosharafa.

Her mission

Moussa believed that nuclear technology should serve peace. She hoped it would make medical treatment cheap enough for everyone, and she took part in the movement for the peaceful use of atomic energy. She organised the Atomic Energy for Peace Conference in Egypt and supported the call for an international "Atoms for Peace" conference.

"I'll make nuclear treatment as available and as cheap as Aspirin."Attributed to Sameera Moussa, as quoted by Physics Today

Key moments

  • 1917. Born in Zifta, Gharbia.
  • Cairo University. Earns her degree, then becomes the first woman to hold a teaching post there.
  • Doctorate. Becomes the first Egyptian woman with a PhD in atomic radiation.
  • Later career. Travels to the United States on a Fulbright scholarship and visits American atomic facilities, which caused debate there. She was offered a chance to stay and work in the US and chose to return to Egypt.
  • August 1952. Dies in a car accident in California at the age of 35. Accounts of her last days are often second-hand, so some details remain disputed.

Why she matters

She opened doors in Egyptian science for women and showed that a researcher can put public good ahead of prestige. Her call for affordable, peaceful uses of nuclear science still speaks to students and engineers today.
